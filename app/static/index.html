Reveal: wind switched off estimated

Nobody publishes waste per farm. We rebuild it two independent ways.

  • Method A (market): each farm tells the grid what it plans to generate (its Physical Notification). When the grid operator pays it to turn down, that is a bid acceptance. Switched off = planned minus what the accepted instructions allowed, half hour by half hour.
  • Method B (physics): wind speed at the hub, run through the turbine power curve, minus what the farm actually metered. It never looks at the market, so where A and B agree, the waste is real.
  • Costs: paid to switch off = waste × the farm's accepted bid price. Replacement gas = waste × (gas price ÷ 2.93 ÷ 50% efficiency + carbon price × 0.37 t/MWh).

Predict: tomorrow's waste forecast

Error = mean absolute error per farm-hour (lower is better). Caught = share of real waste events (over in an hour) the model saw coming. Precision = share of its warnings that were real. Monthly total = error in the month's total waste.

    Simulate: consistency check

    This is a consistency check, not a validation. With today's grid the simulator is built to reproduce history (potential = what was delivered + what was switched off, and waste is shared out the way it was). It confirms the engine and the data agree, so every change you see in a scenario comes from the lever you moved.

    Autopilot: batteries

      Limitations

      • Three zones. North Scotland, South Scotland, and England & Wales as one. Bottlenecks further south are not modelled.
      • Which bottleneck. Public data doesn't say which boundary each turn-down was for. North farms are also turned down when only B6 is full, so we over-credit B4. The simulator inherits this: it overstates what a B4 upgrade saves. The direction is robust; the size is an upper bound.
      • The constraint flag isn't perfect. We count turn-downs the operator marks as system actions. Some ordinary balancing may be in or out.
      • 33 farms, hand-checked (the top Scottish units by constraint volume), coordinates approximate. Smaller farms are in the background "residual".
      • NESO's day-ahead limits have no publication time. We assume they're known by 10:00 the day before; the model without them is almost as good (see above).
      • Batteries are a simple model: no costs, no wear, no effect on prices, and they charge only from wind that would be wasted.

      Data sources

      • Elexon BMRS: planned output (PN), bid acceptances (BOALF), metered output, bid prices.
      • NESO: boundary limits and flows for B4 and B6, demand.
      • Open-Meteo: historical and forecast wind speed at each farm.
