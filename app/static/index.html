Decision log

How every number on this dashboard is made, what it was checked against, and where it is weak.

History: wasted wind power our estimate

Nobody publishes waste per farm. We rebuild it two independent ways.

  • Method A (market): each farm tells the grid what it plans to generate (its Physical Notification). When the grid operator pays it to turn down, that is a bid acceptance. Switched off = planned minus what the accepted instructions allowed, half hour by half hour.
  • Method B (physics): wind speed at hub height through each farm's own power curve, learned from half hours when nobody was turned down, minus what it actually metered. It never uses the turn-down volumes, so where A and B agree, the waste is real.
  • Costs: paid to switch off = waste × the farm's bid price for that half hour. Replacement gas = waste × (gas price ÷ 2.93 ÷ 50% efficiency + carbon price × 0.37 t/MWh).

Forecast: tomorrow's waste forecast

Error = mean absolute error per farm-hour (lower is better). Caught = share of real waste events (over in an hour) the model saw coming. Precision = share of its warnings that were real. Monthly total = error in the month's total waste.

    Simulate: consistency check

    This is a consistency check, not a validation. With today's grid the simulator is built to reproduce history (potential = what was delivered + what was switched off, and waste is shared out the way it was). It confirms the engine and the data agree, so every change you see in a scenario comes from the lever you moved.

    Batteries

      Limitations

      • Three zones. North Scotland, South Scotland, and England & Wales as one. Bottlenecks further south are not modelled.
      • Which bottleneck. Public data doesn't say which boundary each turn-down was for. North farms are also turned down when only B6 is full, so we over-credit B4. The simulator inherits this: it overstates what a B4 upgrade saves. The direction is robust; the size is an upper bound.
      • The constraint flag isn't perfect. We count turn-downs the operator marks as system actions. Some ordinary balancing may be in or out.
      • 33 farms, hand-checked (the top Scottish units by constraint volume), coordinates approximate. Smaller farms are in the background "residual".
      • NESO's day-ahead limits have no publication time. We assume they're known by 10:00 the day before; the model without them is almost as good (see above).
      • Batteries are a simple model: no costs, no wear, no effect on prices, and they charge only from wind that would be wasted. They don't yet sell into spare room on a new undersea cable, so their earnings there are understated.
      • Simulator assumptions. A new undersea cable carries its zone's surplus first and is available 95% of the time on average (no individual outages, no cable losses), so cable results are optimistic. New demand sizes are averages over a full year: heat pumps follow typical Scottish monthly temperatures, not each actual day's, which overstates what they absorb (cold days tend to be calm); EVs charge mostly overnight; data centres are flat. Price controls change only £ and CO₂: a multiplier keeps the farms' order and a flat price treats them all the same, so neither changes which farms are switched off.
      • Batteries at a wind farm can only store the waste of the farms within 20 km (or the nearest one). Every battery's charge is taken out of specific farms' waste, so farm-by-farm results add up to the zone totals. They plan with those farms' historical forecast, which doesn't know about other changes in your scenario (bigger pinch points, more wind, a new cable).

      Data sources

      • Elexon BMRS: planned output (PN), turn-down instructions (BOALF), metered output, bid prices, demand outturn and the day-ahead national demand forecast.
      • NESO: limits and flows for the B4 and B6 pinch points.
      • Open-Meteo: historical wind, archived day-ahead forecasts ("previous runs") and the live forecast at each farm.
